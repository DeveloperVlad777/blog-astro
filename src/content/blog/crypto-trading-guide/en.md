## What Is Crypto Trading?

It involves the process of analyzing and buying/selling operations with cryptocurrency. Many traders use technical analysis, different chart tools, and patterns to predict the time to sell and buy.

But the basis here is not in this process, but in the answer to the question: why are you doing this? Most of the people who chase profits lose in the crypto market and come out with a loss. The most difficult thing is to stay on a long-term period and turn positive. You already understand that this is very different from the traditional asset market, which rarely loses tens of percent per day.

Key elements of the crypto market:

- **Exchanges** — digital marketplaces like Binance, OKX, Coinbase, or Bybit, where buyers and sellers meet.
- **Order types** — market orders execute instantly at the best price; limit orders execute only at a specific price; stop orders help manage losses.
- **Liquidity** — the ease of buying or selling without major price slippage. Highly liquid pairs (e.g., BTC/USDT) trade millions daily.
- **Volatility** — rapid price changes, often larger than in traditional assets. Volatility attracts traders but demands strict risk control.

## Main Types of Crypto Trading

Each trading style fits different experience levels, risk appetites, and goals. Let's explore more about strategies and how to use them.

- **Spot Trading.** You buy and hold a cryptocurrency directly, hoping its value increases. It's the simplest and most beginner-friendly type — no leverage or liquidation risks.
- **Margin Trading.** You borrow funds to increase your position size, magnifying both profits and losses. For instance, trading with 5x leverage means a 10% price change equals a 50% gain or loss. Margin trading requires discipline and a solid risk strategy.
- **Futures and Derivatives.** Traders speculate on future prices using contracts rather than owning assets. Platforms allow both long (price increase) and short (price decrease) positions. This market is large, so in 2025, crypto derivatives accounted for over 65% of all trading volume.
- **P2P and OTC Trading.** Peer-to-peer (P2P) platforms connect buyers and sellers directly, while OTC (over-the-counter) trading handles large institutional orders outside exchanges. Both offer privacy and flexibility but require trust and security checks.
- **Arbitrage.** This strategy exploits price differences across exchanges. For example, buying Bitcoin at $100,950 on one exchange and selling it for $102,400 on another. Arbitrage opportunities are smaller today due to automated bots but still exist in low-liquidity markets.

## Common Trading Strategies

What is the difference between trading and gambling? Attitude. It is important to understand that there are a million strategies; we will analyze only the most used ones.

- **Day Trading** — multiple trades per day, aiming to profit from short-term volatility. Requires active monitoring and technical skills.
- **Swing Trading** — holding positions for days or weeks, targeting larger price swings. A balance between activity and patience.
- **Scalping** — making dozens or hundreds of micro-trades within hours. Demands high focus and fast execution.
- **Position Trading** — long-term holding based on fundamentals, often months or years. Suits investors who trust the project's value.
- **Algorithmic Trading** — using bots or scripts to automate orders based on coded strategies or indicators.
